## How the controls interact

A compressor turns loud moments down so the quiet ones sit closer to them. Three knobs share that job, and they make the most sense read together.

### Sensitivity: what counts as too loud

**Sensitivity** sets the level the compressor starts reacting at. Turn it up and quieter playing gets caught; turn it down and only your hardest hits do. If Dynamics seems to do nothing, Sensitivity is probably too low for how hard you play.

With the **Dusty** dip on, Sensitivity also sets roughly how hard the limiter overdrives. While ramping is engaged, the pedal's own SENSITIVITY knob sets Ramp Speed instead; the editor keeps the two apart.

### Dynamics: how hard to clamp

**Dynamics** sets what happens to anything over that level. Its sweep runs compression → limiting → sag, and the help card for Dynamics names the zone it is in:

- **Compression**, up to noon: loud notes are turned down, lightly at first and steeply by the time you reach noon.
- **Limiting**, from noon: loud notes are held at a ceiling, and past noon the clamp gets firmer still.
- **Sag**, at the top: the sound droops and recovers like an amp running out of headroom. **Dynamics Max** jumps straight here.

### Wet and Dry: making up the gain

Clamping the loud parts leaves the compressed signal quieter overall. **Wet** brings it back up: this is the make-up gain, and it can go past unity. **Dry** blends in the untouched signal, so keeping some Dry gives back the pick attack that heavy settings smooth away. Both run before the final limiter, so even a big boost stays under control.

### Attack and Release: how fast

**Attack** sets how quickly the clamp grabs a loud note: slow lets the start of each note through before it is turned down. **Release** sets how quickly it lets go, with **User Release** taking over when Release is in the middle. Attack also sets how fast the Shifty and Modulated EQ modes move, and the rate of **Motion**.

### A starting point

1. Set Dynamics around nine o'clock, Wet around one o'clock, EQ at noon, and Sensitivity and Dry all the way down.
2. Play at your normal strength and raise Sensitivity until you hear the loudest notes being held back.
3. Raise Dynamics for a firmer hold, then balance Wet against the bypassed level.
4. Bring in Dry if the attack of each note has gone soft.
